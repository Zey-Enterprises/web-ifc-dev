They can, but the label is often part of the problem. "Cheat meal" frames ordinary dietary flexibility as rule-breaking, which can quietly reinforce the idea that you are failing, being unprincipled, or leaving the plan. A better term is something like "planned flexibility", "feast meal", or "higher-calorie meal"—something value-neutral that makes clear the meal is part of the strategy, not a lapse in character.

For fat loss, the main question is still {% include glossary.html id="calorie-balance" %}&#58; Does the meal fit your weekly intake well enough to preserve the intended deficit? One planned restaurant meal can fit. A weekly uncontrolled episode that erases the deficit cannot. Higher-calorie meals can also help adherence by giving you psychological relief, better training energy, and a temporary break from the grind of restriction, but that does not make them magic or unlimited.

Be careful with spiky eating patterns. Saving a huge number of calories all week for an unbounded feast day can create hunger, food preoccupation, poor training, and chaotic {% include glossary.html id="satiety" text="satiety signaling" %}. The better approach is to decide in advance what kind of flexibility your life needs, budget for it, enjoy it deliberately, and return to normal eating afterward. The right structure is the one that keeps the diet both effective and repeatable.
